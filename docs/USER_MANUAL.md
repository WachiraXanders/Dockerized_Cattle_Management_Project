# DairyPro — User Manual

## Getting started

1. Open DairyPro in your browser.
2. **First time ever using it?** Click "First time here? Set up your
   farm" and create your account — you'll automatically become the farm
   admin.
3. **Already have an account?** Enter your email and password and click
   Sign in.

If you don't have an account and someone else set up the farm, ask your
farm admin to invite you (Settings → Users) — you'll get an email, and
you register using that same email address to activate your account.

## Finding your way around

The left-hand menu (or, on a phone, the menu icon in the top corner) is
grouped into sections:

- **Overview** — your Dashboard, the first thing you see.
- **Livestock** — Cattle, Health, Breeding, Milk Production.
- **Operations** — Inventory, Tasks, Suppliers.
- **Business** — Finance, Reports, Predictive Analytics.
- **System** — Settings.

You'll only see the sections your role gives you access to. If something
you expect to see is missing, ask your farm admin to check your role.

There's a light/dark mode switch at the bottom of the menu — use whichever
is easier on your eyes.

## Recording daily milk production

1. Go to **Milk Production**.
2. Click **Log Milk**.
3. Pick the date and the cow.
4. Fill in the liters for whichever milking sessions you have numbers for
   — Morning, Afternoon, Evening. You don't have to fill in all three;
   leave a session blank if you haven't milked yet or don't have the
   number.
5. Click **Log Milk** to save. Each session you filled in is saved as its
   own record.

If your farm has set a price per liter for the current month, DairyPro
automatically records the income from what you just logged — you don't
need to enter that separately. If you see a yellow warning banner saying
no price is set, tell your farm admin — until it's set, the app won't
know how much your milk is worth this month.

## Recording a health event

1. Go to **Health**.
2. Click **Add Health Record**.
3. Pick the cow, the date, and the type of event (vaccination, treatment,
   checkup, etc.).
4. Fill in what you know — diagnosis, treatment given, cost, vet name.
5. If the animal needs a follow-up visit, set a follow-up date — it will
   show up on the Dashboard as a reminder.

## Recording breeding and calving

1. Go to **Breeding**.
2. Click **Add Breeding Record**, pick the cow, and the breeding date.
   DairyPro automatically works out the expected calving date for you.
3. When the calf is actually born, **edit** that same record: set the
   actual calving date and mark the outcome as "Successful."
4. DairyPro will automatically update the mother's record and open a form
   for you to register the new calf — just fill in the calf's tag number
   and save.

Check the **Calving Map** tab to see upcoming and past calvings on a
calendar, and the **Analytics** tab for breeding success rates.

## Managing feed and supplies

1. Go to **Inventory** to see all your stock items and their current levels.
2. When you use feed or supplies, log it one of three ways:
   - **Quick Feed Log** — fastest, just pick the item and how much you used.
   - **Log Consumption** — for feeding a whole group of animals at once.
   - **Feed Ratio (Cow)** — for logging feed given to one specific animal.
3. Check the **Shopping List** tab — DairyPro automatically flags items
   running low and tells you how urgently to reorder.
4. If you want feed given automatically every day for a period (e.g. a
   calf's daily ration), set it up under the **Feeding Schedules** tab —
   DairyPro will log it and deduct stock every day on its own.

## Managing tasks

Go to **Tasks** to see everything that needs doing — assign a task to a
person, set a due date and priority, and mark it done when it's finished.

## Managing money

Go to **Finance** to see:
- **Transactions** — every payment in or out.
- **Category Reports** — where your money is going, and a button to
  automatically pull in feed purchase and vet costs you already logged
  elsewhere, so you don't have to enter them twice.
- **P&L Statement** and **Cash Flow** — how the farm is doing month to
  month.
- **Forecast** — a projection of where your finances are headed.
- **Milk Prices** — set what you're paid per liter each month. This
  matters — DairyPro uses it to work out your milk income everywhere in
  the app.

## Suppliers

Go to **Suppliers** to keep contact details and payment terms for the
people you buy from. If you owe a supplier money (they gave you credit
terms and you haven't paid yet), DairyPro flags it as "Outstanding" so
nothing slips through the cracks.

## Reports

Go to **Reports** for detailed, filterable reports you can export as a
CSV file or a PDF — milk production, feed use, inventory, financials,
health, and an overall performance scorecard, plus a breeding calendar.

## Predictive Analytics

Go to **Predictive Analytics** for AI-assisted insights: cows producing
less milk than expected, recurring health issues worth investigating, and
a 3-month milk production forecast. (This needs your farm admin to have
set up an AI connection — if it's not configured, you'll see a message
explaining that instead of an error.)

## Working without internet

If you lose signal while out in the field, you can still log milk and
health records:

1. You'll see a dark banner at the bottom of the screen saying you're offline.
2. Tap **Quick Log**, fill in the milk or health entry, and save — it's
   stored on your device.
3. Once you're back in range, DairyPro automatically sends everything you
   logged to the farm's records. You'll see a confirmation.

Other parts of the app need a connection to work.

## Settings

If you're a farm admin, go to **Settings** to:
- Update your farm's name, location, and contact details.
- Choose your currency.
- Add or remove custom categories for inventory and finance.
- Invite team members and set their role.
- View the Role Matrix — a table showing exactly what each role can do.

## Getting help

If something looks wrong or you're stuck, check with your farm admin
first — many screens are limited by your role on purpose. If you find an
actual error message, note down what you were doing when it happened
before reporting it.
